# Tombakan — Verified Indonesian Fish Species Data

**Purpose:** Authoritative source for the 8 starter species used in the game.
All Indonesian common names, scientific names, and fun facts are verified against
FishBase, LIPI (Indonesian Institute of Sciences), and KKP (Ministry of Marine Affairs
and Fisheries of Indonesia) references.

---

## Species List

### 1. Ikan Badut
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Indonesian name** | Ikan Badut |
| **Scientific name** | *Amphiprion ocellaris* |
| **English name** | Clownfish / Ocellaris Clownfish |
| **Rarity** | Common |
| **Placeholder color tint** | `#FF6600` (orange) |
| **Habitat** | Terumbu karang (coral reef) — Indo-Pacific including all Indonesian reef systems |
| **Visual appearance** | Vivid orange body with three white bands edged in black; compact, rounded shape; ~9 cm adult length |
| **Fun fact (Indonesian)** | Ikan badut hidup bersama anemon laut dan tidak tersengat tentakelnya karena kulitnya dilapisi lendir khusus. |

**Verification notes:** *Amphiprion ocellaris* is endemic across Indonesia's Coral Triangle.
The Indonesian name "Ikan Badut" (clown fish) is the universally recognised common name in Indonesian. Fun fact is biologically accurate — the mucosal layer and behaviour of gradually acclimating to anemone nematocysts provides immunity.

### 2. Bandeng
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Indonesian name** | Bandeng |
| **Scientific name** | *Chanos chanos* |
| **English name** | Milkfish |
| **Rarity** | Common |
| **Placeholder color tint** | `#C8D8E8` (silvery-blue) |
| **Habitat** | Pesisir dan tambak (coastal and brackish ponds) — found throughout Indonesian coastline |
| **Visual appearance** | Slender, torpedo-shaped silvery body with forked tail; metallic sheen; ~50–70 cm common size |
| **Fun fact (Indonesian)** | Bandeng adalah ikan asli Indonesia yang sudah dibudidayakan di tambak selama ratusan tahun dan dikenal sebagai ikan banyak duri. |

**Verification notes:** *Chanos chanos* is the sole living member of family Chanidae. It is Indonesia's single most culturally significant farmed fish — bandeng presto (pressure-cooked to soften bones) is a national dish. Recognised by KKP as a flagship aquaculture species. Indonesian children will know it by name.

### 3. Kakap Merah
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Indonesian name** | Kakap Merah |
| **Scientific name** | *Lutjanus argentimaculatus* |
| **English name** | Mangrove Red Snapper |
| **Rarity** | Uncommon |
| **Placeholder color tint** | `#E03020` (red) |
| **Habitat** | Mangrove, muara, dan terumbu karang (mangroves, estuaries, and reefs) — throughout Indonesian archipelago |
| **Visual appearance** | Deep red-pink body, large scales, slightly forked tail, prominent dorsal spines; ~40–60 cm |
| **Fun fact (Indonesian)** | Kakap merah bisa hidup di air laut maupun air payau, dan merupakan salah satu ikan favorit nelayan Indonesia karena dagingnya yang lezat. |

**Verification notes:** *Lutjanus argentimaculatus* is the correct Indonesian species — NOT *Lutjanus campechanus* which is Caribbean/Gulf of Mexico and not found in Indonesian waters. *L. argentimaculatus* is listed by FishBase as distributed throughout the Indo-Pacific including all Indonesian seas. The previous wizard entry used the wrong species; this corrects it.

### 4. Kembung
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Indonesian name** | Kembung |
| **Scientific name** | *Rastrelliger kanagurta* |
| **English name** | Indian Mackerel |
| **Rarity** | Common |
| **Placeholder color tint** | `#3A6EA5` (blue-green) |
| **Habitat** | Laut terbuka pesisir (coastal open water) — one of Indonesia's most abundant pelagic fish |
| **Visual appearance** | Streamlined blue-green back with silver belly; faint yellowish lateral stripe; ~20–35 cm; pointed snout |
| **Fun fact (Indonesian)** | Kembung adalah salah satu ikan yang paling banyak ditangkap di Indonesia dan sering dijual di pasar tradisional sebagai lauk sehari-hari. |

**Verification notes:** *Rastrelliger kanagurta* is consistently the top or near-top species in Indonesia's marine catch statistics (KKP annual reports). Known in some regions as "kembung lelaki" or "kembung perempuan" — *R. kanagurta* is the more common of the two Rastrelliger species. Indonesian children encounter this fish daily at markets.

### 5. Tuna Sirip Kuning
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Indonesian name** | Tuna Sirip Kuning |
| **Scientific name** | *Thunnus albacares* |
| **English name** | Yellowfin Tuna |
| **Rarity** | Rare |
| **Placeholder color tint** | `#1A3A6A` (deep blue with yellow accent) — use `#1E4080` |
| **Habitat** | Laut dalam dan samudra (deep sea and ocean) — Indonesia is a global top-5 tuna-producing nation |
| **Visual appearance** | Dark metallic blue-black dorsal surface, silver belly, distinctive bright yellow second dorsal fin and finlets; large, streamlined; 100–180 cm adult |
| **Fun fact (Indonesian)** | Tuna sirip kuning bisa berenang dengan kecepatan hingga 75 km/jam dan Indonesia adalah salah satu negara penghasil tuna terbesar di dunia. |

**Verification notes:** *Thunnus albacares* is Indonesia's primary commercial tuna species. Indonesia's Exclusive Economic Zone covers the Banda Sea, Sulawesi Sea, and Indian Ocean — all major yellowfin grounds. Speed figure (75 km/h) is cited in NOAA and FishBase literature. Rarity set to Rare: appropriate for a large, deep-water species that is exciting to catch in-game.

### 6. Kerapu Bebek
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Indonesian name** | Kerapu Bebek |
| **Scientific name** | *Cromileptes altivelis* |
| **English name** | Humpback Grouper / Polka-dot Grouper |
| **Rarity** | Uncommon |
| **Placeholder color tint** | `#F0EEE0` (pale cream/white) |
| **Habitat** | Terumbu karang (coral reefs) — endemic to the Indo-Pacific; high-value in Indonesian aquaculture |
| **Visual appearance** | White or pale grey body covered densely with black polka dots; distinctive humped back (high dorsal profile); ~50–70 cm |
| **Fun fact (Indonesian)** | Kerapu bebek punya bintik-bintik hitam di seluruh tubuhnya dan merupakan ikan karang paling mahal di Indonesia karena rasanya sangat enak. |

**Verification notes:** *Cromileptes altivelis* is one of Indonesia's most economically valuable reef fish — it commands premium prices in live fish trade to Hong Kong/China. "Bebek" (duck) in the name refers to its distinctive humped snout profile. Found in Indonesian reef systems across Sulawesi, Maluku, and Papua. Visually very distinct (white + black dots) making it ideal for a children's game.

### 7. Lele
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Indonesian name** | Lele |
| **Scientific name** | *Clarias batrachus* |
| **English name** | Walking Catfish |
| **Rarity** | Common |
| **Placeholder color tint** | `#4A4040` (dark grey-brown) |
| **Habitat** | Air tawar: sungai, kolam, dan sawah (freshwater: rivers, ponds, rice paddies) — ubiquitous across Java, Sumatra, Kalimantan |
| **Visual appearance** | Dark grey to brownish-black elongated body; flat broad head; long whisker barbels (4 pairs); no scales; ~30–45 cm common size |
| **Fun fact (Indonesian)** | Lele bisa berjalan di darat menggunakan siripnya untuk berpindah ke kolam lain ketika airnya kering — itulah mengapa disebut "walking catfish"! |

**Verification notes:** *Clarias batrachus* is native to Southeast Asia including Indonesia and is the species behind Indonesia's enormous lele (catfish) aquaculture industry — it is farmed in millions of backyard ponds (kolam lele) across Java. It genuinely can survive briefly out of water using a modified accessory breathing organ. Every Indonesian child knows "lele" and "pecel lele" (a national street food). Fun fact is accurate.

### 8. Ikan Nila
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Indonesian name** | Ikan Nila |
| **Scientific name** | *Oreochromis niloticus* |
| **English name** | Nile Tilapia |
| **Rarity** | Common |
| **Placeholder color tint** | `#556B55` (olive green) |
| **Habitat** | Air tawar: danau, waduk, sungai, dan kolam budidaya (freshwater: lakes, reservoirs, rivers, and fish ponds) — introduced and now dominant throughout Indonesia |
| **Visual appearance** | Laterally compressed oval body; olive-green to grey with faint vertical bars on flanks; reddish tinge on dorsal fin; ~25–40 cm |
| **Fun fact (Indonesian)** | Ikan nila berasal dari Afrika tapi sekarang menjadi salah satu ikan air tawar yang paling banyak dibudidayakan di Indonesia. |

**Verification notes:** *Oreochromis niloticus* was introduced to Indonesia in 1969 and is now the dominant freshwater aquaculture species after lele. KKP statistics consistently place nila in the top 3 freshwater aquaculture species by volume. Although originally from the Nile basin, it is now inseparably associated with Indonesian freshwater food production — children know it well. Fun fact is accurate and the origin story (from Africa) is age-appropriately interesting.

## Notes for Artists

- All species use the **existing tuna mesh + baseColor tint** as placeholder until dedicated models are created.
- Priority for custom models: Ikan Badut (most visually distinct, silhouette recognition), Kerapu Bebek (spots need texture), Lele (barbels, flat head — very different silhouette from tuna).
- Kerapu Bebek's pale/white tint (`#F0EEE0`) may be hard to read against a bright AR background — consider adding a secondary spot texture overlay at art pass.
- Tuna Sirip Kuning is the only Rare marine species, suitable as a "wow catch" moment.
